AuraLoom

  
A picture of sound you can play. Left to right is time (the faint columns are 16th notes, brighter ones are beats and bars). Bottom to top is pitch: every row is a quarter-tone and every octave is the same height, like a piano turned on its side. Brightness is loudness. Press Space or ▶ and the loom plays as a loop; keep painting while it plays and you'll hear the change on the next pass.

  
Threads (brushes)

  
    
    
  • Tone: a held note. Drag sideways to hold it; drag up or down to glide.
    • 
    
  • Pluck: a struck note that rings and fades. Click for one; drag to strum. Ring sets how long it rings.
    • 
    
  • Kick, Snare, Hat: drums. Click where you want the hit; with Grid ⊞ on they land on the nearest 16th.
    • 
    
  • Noise: a soft smear of hiss, breath or wind. Noise threads show in cyan.
    • 
    
  • Erase: unpick anything under the brush. Size sets its width.
    • 
  

  
Voice, key and scale

  
Voice is the overtone recipe for Tone and Pluck: Pure is a single thread, String and Reed stack harmonics above it (you'll see the ladder), Bell uses the out-of-tune partials of a real bell. The Genesis FM voices are two-operator FM, the trick behind the Mega Drive's sound chip: the brightness moves over the life of each note (a bass that starts with a bark, brass that swells), and you can watch the sidebands open and close on the loom. Crunch rounds the output to fewer bits, like a console DAC. Key and Scale snap your notes to the scale; the in-key rows are lit and the root is cyan. Free turns snapping off.

  
Clean and Ghost

  
    
    
  • Clean: every tone row is a smooth oscillator, so painted notes are crisp and in tune. Noise threads become real noise.
    • 
    
  • Ghost: the whole picture is rebuilt the way an AuraGraph tile plays back, with Griffin-Lim (1984): start with random phase, then go back and forth between sound and picture until they agree. It sounds like a memory of the music. Use it for imported recordings and pictures.
    • 
  

  
Bringing things in

  
    
    
  • ♫ Audio: weaves the first stretch of a recording onto the loom (as long as your loop). Paint over it, play it in Ghost, compare with ▶ Original.
    • 
    
  • 🖼 Picture: reads any image as sound. Top is high, bright is loud. Try a photo, a drawing or your handwriting.
    • 
    
  • ◫ AuraGraph tile: loads a tile made by AuraGraph or Paint-a-Tile using its real pitch axis (30 Hz to 11 kHz), so the notes land where they belong. Clips work too: a piece cut out of a tile carries its own bounds (inside the PNG, or in its name, like _t12.3-13.1_f200-800), so the loop becomes exactly that many seconds and the notes land at their real pitch. Stretch it to hear it slowly.
    • 
  

  
Scores (text)

  
Open ✎ Score to write music as text and Weave it, or press ⇡ Write score to turn what's on the loom into text: the last woven score plus every stroke you've painted since (erasing included). Edit it, weave it back, or share it as a link. Imported recordings and pictures can't become a score, because a score holds threads, not pictures.

  
Stretch and slow listening

  
Stretch × widens (above 1) or pinches (below 1) the whole loom in time. Pitch never moves, so it's a lens for looking at a sound's shape in slow motion: the rivers of its harmonics, the valleys between them, how the energy pours in at an attack and drains away. Painted threads are redrawn sharp at the new tempo; imported pictures and recordings are resampled, and past ×4 that fills in between the columns rather than adding detail. Loops can run from 0.5 s to 120 s. Undo takes it back.

  
Listen plays the loop at ½, ¼ or ⅛ speed with the pitch unchanged, without touching the loom. Changing BPM on painted work re-weaves it from its threads, so nothing blurs.

  
Taking things out

  
WAV is one pass of the loop with the Space reverb ringing out at the end. Loop WAV is exactly one loop long with that tail already folded over the start, so it repeats without a seam in any player, editor or game engine. PNG is the loom as you see it. Tile is an AuraGraph-format tile any crew member can read. Project saves everything to reopen later.
